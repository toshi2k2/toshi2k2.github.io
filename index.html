Cognitive AI · 3D vision & graphics

Prakhar Kaushik

My research brings principles from cognitive science and cognitive computing into 3D computer vision, computer graphics, and interpretable AI. I develop structured representations of objects, scenes, and learned knowledge to make visual intelligence more human-like. I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at Johns Hopkins University, advised by Alan Yuille.

A central thread is 3D part-based understanding: connecting object geometry with semantic meaning. In Name That Part, we jointly segment and name 3D object parts. This focus on structured representations also shapes my reconstruction work: FurE recovers editable animal fur from images without animal-fur training datasets, while Honeycomb-Surfels uses compact surface primitives for efficient scene rendering and reconstruction.

I also investigate the limits of visual understanding. My work on multiview consistency shows how 3D foundation models can hallucinate geometry from unrelated images or random noise, and develops metrics to detect such failures.

A complementary direction examines how networks learn and retain knowledge. The Universal Weight Subspace Hypothesis studies shared low-dimensional structure across trained models, connecting my interests in explainable AI and the physics of AI with efficient adaptation and continual learning. Across these directions, my goal is more human-like intelligence that is efficient, robust, and interpretable.
